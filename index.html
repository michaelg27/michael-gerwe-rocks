Dungeons & Dragons Loico 2026 Open

After watching a playthrough of the game Don't Look Outside, I was so inspired that I felt the utter need to make that into a DND setting. The core concept of the campaign was that they were stuck inside a castle that was eldritchly warped by the waking of a long asleep god, and I'm proud of the setting I made. Mechanically, I think it was very clunky while playing the game in terms of exploration, because everything is stuck indoors which puts many limits on how the players can travel and find information. I reckon that the clunkyness resulted in me making up a lot of details of the world, much more so than usual. Either way, it was a super fun challenge, and I'm really grateful my players enjoyed it. I knew I wanted to play with themes of religious obsession, fanaticism, and what exactly does it mean to have faith in a organisation that has been party to a lot of corruption in the world. I find that DND is a really powerful vehicle for these themes due to how it handles the practice of magic and what it means to be a cleric (someone who gets their magic threw the worship of a god), a warlock (someone who makes a deal with a god), or a sorcerer (someone who is just born with their magic). I find divinity really interesting, and I'm grateful that this was a really cool opportunity to build a theocratic society where my players could grapple with those ideas.

Dungeons & Dragons Epoch 2024 Open

Campaign wise, I wasn't the best dm for this world since the players just kept guessing my ending, so I kept changing it (stupid). The energy over the sessions we had just kind of sizzled out, making the campaign not that fun for anyone anymore. World wise, this is one of my best. I personally think that I took everything that went wrong with Epoch as a world, and improved on it. I love this world to bits. I wanted to play with themes of espionage, political intrigue, and what it really means to be a good leader. It's sci-fi, and so much more. I ended up using this world a lot of times while volunteering to run games for middle schoolers later after the failure of my home game in this world. Point is, I really like this setting and what I'm able to play with when I use it.

Dungeons & Dragons Space Campaign 2023 Open

This campaign was fire! I'm speaking about a lot of these with rose-tinted glasses, but man this campaign was so much fun. The same player that had the idea to drink the god's blood in Nokocord went absolutely feral in this campaign. Spelljammer (the space supplement for DND) just released, D20's Starstruck Odyssey was in the middle of airing, and it was so much fun. I basically copy and pasted this world (not well) from Critical Role's Explorer's Guide to Wildemount which was a fun experience, but added space to it! We were in 8th grade, life was stress free, I was young, childish, and brash. I sometimes miss that time, when life was all about having your friends and doing whatever crazy stuff you could. Point is, I really liked this campaign. It was my first ever sci-fi world, and we just had such a blast playing this game. I still have the miniature one of the players bought and hand painted for the final boss specifically. World-building wise, it's alright. I put a lot of work into it, but I know I've made way better worlds. That's not what makes DND so special, though. This is a campaign I try to look back to a lot of the time in my dming, and I don't think I live up to it. When I say the vibes were immaculate, that's what made that campaign so special. Not the mediocre world or the very new idea (to me) of sci-fi DND but the players are what it so awesome. Damn.

Dungeons & Dragons Nokocord 2022 Open

My first ever campaign I'm really proud of. I was in seventh grade when I wrote this, and I'm so proud of how far I've come. I'd run like small things before, but this campaign was my first world that really felt good playing and that I was really proud of. This was the campaign that made DND click for me, and I'm really grateful to my friends who put up with it. If I had to describe the setting, the closest thing I can liken it to is magical pirates. That's actually not that inaccurate. Most notably though, this was the first campaign where a player really ruined my plans. They essentially were meant to use god's blood as an ingredient for this ritual that was the main focus of the campaign. Cool idea. Go me. What ended up happening was that they kill this demigod, and rather than storing the god's blood in a safe place for the ritual, they just drink it! THEY DRINK ONE OF THE MAIN INGREDIENTS OF THE RITUAL! What incredible creativity, and I didn't know what to do. I ended up giving them 10 levels in Warlock because that's so absurd, but that ended up screwing with a lot of their character builds in a way that was narratively and mechanically unsatisfying in a way the players really liked. Oh good times. Thank you Nokocord, for the path you set me down.
